# Run discounts and coupons

Two different tools that are easy to confuse:

- **Campaigns** apply automatically. The shopper sees a lower price without doing anything.
- **Coupons** need a code. The shopper types it at checkout.

Use a campaign for a sale everyone should get. Use a coupon when you want to control who gets it, or
to find out where your customers came from.

## Campaigns

Campaigns *Schedule storewide, category, or product discount campaigns.* Choose the scope — the whole
store, one category, one sub-category, a tag, or specific products you pick yourself — set the
discount, and set the dates.

Scheduling is the useful part: set the start and end in advance and the campaign begins and ends on
its own. No one has to remember to switch it off at midnight, which is exactly when everyone forgets.

Set the end date when you create it, even for something you intend to run indefinitely. A forgotten
campaign is a permanent price cut you are not aware of.

### Every campaign has its own page to share

Each campaign gets its own page in your shop, listing exactly the products it discounts. Press
**Copy link** on the campaign's row and paste it into a Facebook post, a boosted ad, or a message to
a customer.

This matters most for a sale you built by hand. If you picked twenty products out of eight different
categories, no category link covers them — the campaign's own link is the only address that shows a
shopper those twenty products and nothing else.

The link works as soon as you save the campaign, so you can announce a sale before it opens: until
the start date the page shows the usual prices and tells shoppers when the sale begins. It stops
working the day the campaign ends, which is deliberate — a live link after a finished sale would show
full prices under a banner promising a discount.

### Designing that page

By default the campaign's page shows the sale's name, the discount, how long is left, and the
products on offer. That is enough for most sales.

If you want more — customer reviews, a video, your own headline, answers to common questions — tick
**Create a page for this campaign** when you create it. You get a page you can edit like any other, at the same link,
starting with exactly what it showed before. Add sections above and below the products and publish.

Changed your mind later? The campaign's row has a button that opens its page, creating it first if
there isn't one. Your campaign pages are listed in **Pages** under **Campaign pages**, and deleting a
campaign deletes its page with it.

Two smaller things worth knowing:

- **The Tagline field** on the campaign is the line printed under the name on that page. One short
  sentence — "Three days only, on every cushion" — is enough.
- **Renaming a campaign does not change its link.** Anything you have already shared keeps working.

### The strip that announces it

While a campaign is running, your shop shows a strip under the header naming the sale and linking to
its page. It appears and disappears with the campaign's dates on its own — you never switch it on for a
sale or off after one.

How it looks is yours to set, in **Customize → Campaign strip**: background and text colour, text
size, how much space it takes, whether shoppers can close it, and whether it shows on desktop,
mobile, or both. You can also keep it to the home page only, though leaving it on every page is
usually better — a shopper reading a product page is the one deciding whether to buy.

Nothing in that panel changes the sale itself. It cannot start a campaign, extend one, or bring back
one that has ended; the dates on the campaign decide that. If the strip is missing when you expect
it, check the campaign's dates first — an ended campaign has nothing to announce.

## Coupons

Coupons let you *Create and manage storefront discount codes.* Each has a code the shopper enters,
a discount, and usually limits — an expiry date, a usage cap, or a minimum order value.

Limits are not optional in practice. A code with no cap and no expiry will eventually be posted
somewhere public and used far more than you planned. A minimum order value also stops a coupon meant
to encourage a big basket being spent on the cheapest thing you sell.

Different codes for different places — one for your Facebook page, one for a flyer — tell you which
channel actually brings in orders.

## Check the margin first

Both tools discount the price the shopper pays; neither changes what the stock cost you. Before
setting a rate, check the margin on what you are discounting — see
[Read your reports](./read-your-reports.md). A 20% discount on a 15% margin is a sale you lose money
on, and volume makes it worse rather than better.

These affect online prices only. Counter discounts are set separately — see
[Set up discounts and VAT](./discounts-and-vat.md).

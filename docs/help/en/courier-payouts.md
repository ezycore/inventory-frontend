# Record courier payments

When a courier delivers a cash-on-delivery order, the shopper pays the rider at the door. The courier
keeps that money for a few days, then pays you — usually one payment covering many parcels, minus
their charges.

**Courier Payouts** shows what each courier owes you right now, and is where you record the money
when it arrives. It works the same way for every courier: Pathao, Steadfast, eCourier, and your own
delivery partners.

## Only if you track your money

Recording courier payments is part of the **Accounts** feature. If Accounts is off, the screen is
called **Courier charges** and shows only what each courier really billed for delivery against what
you charged your customers. There is no "owed to you" figure and no payment to record — a delivered
parcel simply counts as paid. Turn Accounts on in Settings → Customize workspace when you want to
check every courier payment.

## What happens when a parcel is delivered

As soon as the courier reports a COD parcel **delivered** — or you mark your own delivery partner's
parcel delivered — the order is settled automatically:

- the order reads **Paid**, because the shopper has paid;
- the money is recorded as **held by that courier**, not as cash in your account;
- the shopper is never shown as owing you anything. A COD shopper either pays the rider or refuses the
  parcel — online orders are never a customer debt.

While a parcel is still on its way, its payment shows as **COD pending**.

If the courier reports a **partial delivery** (the shopper kept only part, or paid less), nothing is
settled automatically. The order asks you to record what was actually collected, with **Collected a
different amount…** on the order.

## What the screen shows

One card per courier:

- **Owed to you** — for every parcel the courier has not paid you for yet: what they collected, less
  their charge. A returned parcel still costs its delivery charge, so it lowers the figure. If the
  number is negative, you owe the courier.
- **Oldest** — how long the oldest parcel has been waiting. Most couriers pay within two to seven
  days, so anything older than a week is worth a phone call.
- **Short from earlier payments** — if a payment came in lower than expected, the difference stays
  here until it is paid or written off.

Below the cards: **Quoted vs actual** (what delivery was quoted at dispatch against what the courier
really billed) and your payment history.

The owed figure is only as good as each parcel's charge. Where the courier has not told us its real
charge yet, the dispatch quote is used and the parcel says the charge is an estimate.

## Recording a payment

When money arrives, press **Record payment received** on that courier's card.

1. **Amount received** — what actually arrived in your bank or bKash.
2. **Date** — leave it for today.
3. **Received into** — the account the money landed in.
4. **Parcels this covers** — ticked for you, oldest first, until they add up to the amount. Change the
   ticks if the courier's statement lists different parcels.
5. **Reference** (optional) — the courier's statement number or the bKash TrxID.

The form shows what the ticked parcels should come to, what you received, and the difference. Press
**Record payment** and the money moves into your account, the courier's charges are recorded as
delivery costs, and the parcels leave the card.

## When the amount does not match

A difference never stops you saving.

**Short.** The form asks what the missing amount is:

- **Extra courier charges** — the courier took more than the parcels' charges say (a COD fee,
  re-weighing). It is recorded as delivery cost on this payment and the payment matches. It is not
  split across the parcels, because nothing says which parcel it belongs to. If you do know — the
  courier's statement lists each parcel's fee — correct that order's courier cost before recording
  the payment instead.
- **Still owed** — the courier will pay it later. It stays on the courier's card as **Short from
  earlier payments**. When the rest arrives, record it as another payment with no parcels ticked — it
  counts against the shortfall first. If the courier will never pay it, press **Write off
  shortfall**. This works with Cash & Bank switched off too.

**More than expected.** Usually this is for parcels you shipped from the courier's own panel, which
this shop never recorded. The extra is recorded as an adjustment.

## What you see on an order

An order sent by courier shows its courier charges — what was quoted, what the courier actually
billed, and where the figure came from. If the courier publishes charges, **Ask the courier** fetches
the current one.

Below it, the order says whether the courier still owes you for it, or that a payment has covered it.

# Reconcile courier payouts

When a courier delivers a cash-on-delivery order, they collect your money at the door — and then
they keep it for a few days. When they finally pay it over, they send one payment covering many
parcels, minus their charges.

**Courier Payouts** is where that money is tracked: what is still out with your couriers, what they
charged you for delivering, and what has actually reached your account.

## Why this screen exists

Two things used to go wrong quietly.

**Your cash looked higher than it was.** The moment you recorded a collection, the money counted as
cash in hand — even though the courier was still holding it and would not pay it over for days. On a
COD shop that can be most of a week's takings.

**Your delivery cost was a guess.** The charge recorded against an order was the price quoted when
you sent the parcel. Couriers re-weigh parcels and add a fee for handling your cash, so the real bill
is often higher — sometimes much higher. If your delivery charge to the shopper was based on the
quote, you were losing money on deliveries without it showing anywhere.

## What the screen shows

**With the couriers** — how much each courier is holding, across how many parcels, and **how long**
they have held the oldest one. Most couriers in Bangladesh remit within two to seven days, so
anything older than a week is worth a phone call.

Underneath it lists your clearing accounts. There is one per courier: a holding place for money that
has been collected but not handed over. It is not spendable cash, which is why it is kept out of your
cash total on the accounts page and in your reports — you will see it there as **With Courier**.

**Quoted vs actual** — what delivery was quoted at dispatch against what the courier really billed,
and what that leaves of the shipping you charged shoppers. If the margin is negative, you are paying
to deliver. The "furthest off" list shows the worst offenders.

Parcels where the courier has not told us their charge yet are counted separately, not as accurate.
Steadfast, for example, does not publish a per-parcel charge at all, so those stay on the quote until
a payout settles them.

**Payouts in this period** — what your couriers have kept, split by kind, with two counts worth
watching: how many payouts are waiting for you to confirm, and how many did not add up.

## Recording a payout

Most of the time you do not have to. Press **Check for payouts** and the system asks each courier
what they have paid you. Steadfast reports its payments directly; for the others, the payment is
assembled from the parcels themselves.

To enter one by hand, press **Record a payout** and copy the statement as the courier wrote it:

- **Collected (gross)** — everything they collected, before their charges.
- **What the courier kept** — their charges, line by line. Keep the payment charge separate from
  delivery: it is the fee for sending you the money, not the cost of shipping, and mixing them makes
  your delivery cost look worse than it is.
- **Paid over (net)** — what actually arrived.

The screen checks the arithmetic as you type. If the statement does not add up, it says so — and that
is worth looking into before you save it, because it usually means a line was missed.

Recording a payout does not move any money. It only files what the courier says.

## Confirming the money

Open a payout and press **Confirm & post**. You will be asked which account the money landed in —
your bank, your bKash, whichever received it — and shown exactly what the courier kept before you
agree to it.

Posting does three things at once: moves the net into the account you chose, records the courier's
charges as expenses, and finally books the real delivery cost against the orders involved. After
that, the courier's clearing account should be back to zero for those parcels.

This step is deliberately yours to take. Nothing can know which of your accounts a payment arrived
in, and the charges are worth seeing before they become expenses in your books.

## When a payout does not add up

Two things get flagged, and neither is stopped:

**Money left over.** The parcels in the payout were holding more than the statement accounts for. The
difference stays in the clearing account rather than disappearing — it is money the courier still
owes you, or a parcel they have not reported yet.

**Parcels that are not yours.** If you also ship from the courier's own panel, their payment covers
parcels this shop never recorded. Those are shown as "not one of ours" and recorded as an adjustment.
It is normal, not an error.

A payout marked **Unreconciled** is not broken — it is one to look at.

## What you see on an order

Open any order sent by courier and you will find its courier charges: what was quoted, what the
courier actually billed, and where the figure came from. If the courier publishes charges, **Ask the
courier** fetches the current one and corrects your books if it has changed.

Below it, the order says whether its cash is still with the courier, or which payout brought it in.
